What Are Air Yards?

Air Yards measure the depth of a passing opportunity before the receiver gains any yards after the catch.

Definition

Air Yards = Distance a Pass Travels From the Line of Scrimmage to Its Target

Air Yards describe the depth of an intended pass before the receiver catches the football or the pass falls incomplete. They do not include yards gained after the catch.

How Air Yards Are Calculated

Every target contributes positive, zero, or negative Air Yards depending on where the football is thrown.

01

Downfield Target

A pass targeted 20 yards beyond the line of scrimmage produces 20 Air Yards for the intended receiver.

02

Short Target

A pass thrown five yards beyond the line of scrimmage produces five Air Yards.

03

Behind-the-Line Target

Screens and passes targeted behind the line of scrimmage can produce negative Air Yards.

04

Incomplete Pass

The intended receiver can still receive Air Yards even when the pass is not completed because the opportunity still occurred.

Air Yards vs Target Share

Target volume and target depth measure different parts of a receiver's opportunity.

Volume + Depth

Target Share Measures How Often a Player Is Targeted. Air Yards Measure How Deep Those Targets Travel.

A receiver can command a large percentage of the passing offense while primarily receiving short targets, or earn fewer targets that carry significantly more downfield value. Compare Air Yards with NFL target share leaders to understand both volume and depth of opportunity.

Air Yards vs Receiving Yards

The two metrics describe different parts of the same passing play.

Air Yards

Measure how far the pass travels before reaching the intended receiver.

Receiving Yards

Measure the total yards credited after a completed catch, including yards gained after the catch.

Yards After Catch

Measure the portion of receiving yards gained after the receiver secures the football.

Incomplete Opportunity

Air Yards preserve evidence of downfield opportunity even when a target does not become a reception.

Why Air Yards Matter

Air Yards help separate target quantity from the potential value of those targets.

01

Downfield Opportunity

Higher Air Yards indicate that an offense is consistently creating deeper receiving opportunities.

02

Role Identification

Air Yards help distinguish vertical threats, intermediate receivers, and short-area options.

03

Hidden Opportunity

A receiver can post limited production while still earning valuable downfield targets that simply did not connect.

04

Volatility Context

Deeper targets can create larger outcomes while also producing more game-to-game variability.

Total Air Yards vs Average Depth of Target

Total downfield volume and average target depth should be evaluated together.

Total Air Yards

Add all target distances together to measure total downfield opportunity over a game or sample.

Average Depth of Target

aDOT divides total Air Yards by targets to estimate the typical depth of each opportunity.

High Volume, Shorter Depth

A receiver can accumulate significant targets while primarily operating in short and intermediate areas.

Lower Volume, Deep Role

A receiver can receive fewer targets while still carrying substantial downfield upside.

Air Yards Example

Equal target totals do not always represent equal receiving opportunity.

Example

Receiver A Records 120 Air Yards While Receiver B Records 35

Receiver A is being used farther downfield and may need fewer catches to produce a large receiving-yardage result. Receiver B may operate closer to the line of scrimmage and depend more heavily on reception volume and yards after the catch.

What Changes a Player's Air Yards?

Target depth is influenced by player role, quarterback tendencies, offensive design, and defensive coverage.

01

Route Tree

Go routes, posts, corners, and deep crossers create more Air Yards than screens and short curls.

02

Quarterback Tendencies

Arm strength, aggressiveness, timing, and accuracy influence how often deeper targets are attempted.

03

Offensive Scheme

Play calling and formation design determine which receivers are given vertical opportunities.

04

Defensive Coverage

Safety depth, cornerback leverage, and coverage structure can encourage or limit downfield passing.

NFL Air Yards FAQ

Common questions about Air Yards, aDOT, and downfield receiving opportunity.

What are Air Yards in football?

Air Yards measure the distance a pass travels forward from the line of scrimmage to the intended receiver.

Who leads the NFL in Air Yards?

The PlayMaker leaderboard above tracks current NFL Air Yards leaders using the latest available weekly research data.

What is aDOT?

aDOT stands for average depth of target. It measures the average number of Air Yards attached to each target.

Do incomplete passes count as Air Yards?

Yes. Air Yards measure intended downfield opportunity, so an incomplete target can still contribute Air Yards.

What is the difference between Air Yards and receiving yards?

Air Yards measure target depth before the catch. Receiving yards measure the final yardage produced on completed receptions, including yards after the catch.

Why do Air Yards matter for player props?

Air Yards help show the quality and depth of receiving opportunities, including valuable downfield targets that may not appear in a player's final box-score production.
