NFL Carry Share Leaders 2026 RB Carry Share & Rushing Workload Stats

NFL carry share measures the percentage of a team's rushing attempts handled by a specific player. Use this 2026 NFL carry share guide to understand RB carry share, compare rushing workload, identify backfield control, track role changes, and evaluate how stable a player's rushing opportunity may be.

What Is Carry Share?

Carry share measures how much of a team's rushing workload belongs to one player.

Definition

Carry Share = Player Rush Attempts ÷ Team Rush Attempts

If a running back records 18 carries while the team records 24 total rushing attempts, that player owns a 75% carry share.

How NFL Carry Share Is Calculated

Carry share converts raw rushing attempts into a percentage of total team rushing volume.

01

Count Player Carries

Start with the number of rushing attempts credited to the player over the selected game or sample.

02

Count Team Carries

Measure the team's total rushing attempts over the same sample.

03

Divide Player by Team

Divide the player's rushing attempts by total team rushing attempts.

04

Convert to Percentage

Multiply the result by 100 to express workload as a carry-share percentage.

Carry Share Formula Example

Team rushing volume provides important context around a player's raw number of carries.

Example

18 Player Carries ÷ 24 Team Carries = 75% Carry Share

A 75% carry share means the running back handled three out of every four team rushing attempts. Even if another back scores a touchdown, the larger share can indicate the more repeatable rushing role.

Carry Share vs Raw Carries

Two running backs can record the same number of carries while holding very different roles within their offenses.

Team Context

Ten Carries Can Represent Very Different Backfield Roles

A running back with 10 carries on a team that records 15 total rushing attempts owns a 66.7% carry share. A back with 10 carries on a team with 30 rushing attempts owns only a 33.3% share. Raw volume is equal, but backfield control is not.

What Changes a Player's Carry Share?

Rushing workload can move quickly when injuries, coaching decisions, quarterback usage, or game environment change.

01

Injuries

An injured or inactive teammate can immediately increase another player's expected rushing workload.

02

Coaching Rotation

Some teams divide early-down, passing-down, and goal-line work between multiple running backs.

03

Quarterback Rushing

Mobile quarterbacks can reduce running back carry share through designed runs and scrambling opportunities.

04

Game Environment

Leading teams may increase rushing volume while trailing teams can reduce traditional running back carries.

Passing

Early

NFL Carry Share FAQ

Common questions about running back carry share and rushing workload.

What is carry share in football?

Carry share measures the percentage of a team's rushing attempts handled by a specific player.

How is NFL carry share calculated?

Divide a player's rushing attempts by the team's total rushing attempts over the same sample, then multiply by 100.

What does a high carry share mean?

A high carry share generally means the player controls a large percentage of the team's rushing workload.

Is carry share the same as snap share?

No. Snap share measures playing time. Carry share measures how much of the team's rushing workload a player receives.

Why does carry share matter for running back props?

A larger share of team rushing attempts creates more opportunities for carries, rushing yards, and potentially scoring chances.

Can a running back have high carry share but low rushing yards?

Yes. Carry share measures opportunity, not efficiency. A player can control the rushing workload while producing limited yardage against a difficult matchup or inefficient rushing environment.
