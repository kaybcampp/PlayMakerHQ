What is EPA? Expected Points Added Explained

EPA (Expected Points Added) measures how much a single play increases or decreases a team’s expected scoring outcome.

EPA Definition

The most important efficiency metric in modern NFL analytics.

Definition

EPA = change in expected points caused by a play.

Positive EPA means the play improved scoring chances. Negative EPA means it hurt them.

Why EPA Matters for Props

It measures real impact — not just raw stats.

01

True Efficiency Signal

Shows how valuable a player is per play, not just per game.

02

Game Control Indicator

High EPA offenses sustain drives and increase volume stability.

03

Better Than Yards

EPA accounts for context like down, distance, and field position.

04

Predictive Value

More stable long-term indicator than box score production.

Real NFL Example

Why EPA reveals hidden team quality.

Example

High EPA Drive vs Low EPA Drive

A team can gain yards but still have low EPA if they fail in key situations. EPA captures whether those yards actually mattered for scoring.
