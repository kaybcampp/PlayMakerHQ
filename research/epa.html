What Is EPA in Football? Expected Points Added Explained

EPA, or Expected Points Added, measures how much a single football play increases or decreases a team's expected scoring outcome. It is one of the most widely used efficiency metrics in modern NFL analytics because it evaluates what a play accomplished within the context of the game.

What Does EPA Mean in Football?

EPA stands for Expected Points Added and measures the change in expected scoring value created by a play.

Definition

EPA = Expected Points After the Play − Expected Points Before the Play

Positive EPA means the play improved the offense's expected scoring outcome. Negative EPA means the play reduced it. A play near the goal line, on third down, or in difficult field position can therefore carry different value than the same number of yards gained in another situation.

How Is EPA Calculated?

EPA compares the expected scoring value of the game state before and after each play.

01

Estimate Expected Points Before the Play

An expected-points model estimates how many points an offense is expected to score from its current game situation.

02

Run the Play

The result changes field position, down, distance, possession, clock situation, or scoring outcome.

03

Estimate Expected Points After the Play

The model recalculates the offense's expected scoring value from the new game state.

04

Calculate the Difference

The difference between the new expected-points value and the old value becomes the EPA credited to the play.

EPA Formula

The basic EPA formula is simple even though the expected-points model underneath it can be complex.

Formula

EPA = EP After Play − EP Before Play

If an offense has 1.8 expected points before a play and 2.7 expected points afterward, the play generated +0.9 EPA. If the expected-points value falls from 1.8 to 0.9, the play generated −0.9 EPA.

EPA Football Example

Why identical yardage totals can have completely different values.

Example A

8 Yards on 3rd-and-5

An eight-yard gain converts the first down, extends the drive, and can meaningfully increase expected scoring value.

Example B

8 Yards on 3rd-and-15

The same eight-yard gain may still force a punt and therefore provide much less value to the offense.

Context

Same Yardage, Different EPA

EPA distinguishes between the two plays because down and distance determine how much each gain changes the expected scoring outcome.

Takeaway

Context Matters

Raw yards describe what happened. EPA helps describe how valuable the result was within the game situation.

What Is Offensive EPA?

Offensive EPA measures how much expected scoring value an offense creates through its plays.

Offensive Efficiency

Higher offensive EPA generally means an offense is creating more scoring value.

Offensive EPA can be evaluated at the play, drive, player, game, or team level. Positive offensive EPA indicates that the offense is consistently improving its scoring position, while negative EPA indicates that plays are reducing expected scoring value.

What Is Defensive EPA?

Defensive EPA measures how much expected scoring value a defense allows opposing offenses to generate.

Lower

Strong Defensive EPA Allowed

Defenses that consistently limit expected scoring value can force inefficient drives and difficult down-and-distance situations.

Higher

Weak Defensive EPA Allowed

Defenses allowing greater scoring value per play may create more favorable offensive environments.

Pass

EPA Against the Pass

Passing EPA allowed helps evaluate how efficiently opposing quarterbacks and receivers create value.

Run

EPA Against the Run

Rushing EPA allowed helps evaluate whether a defense consistently limits or enables valuable rushing outcomes.

What Is EPA Per Play?

EPA per play normalizes total EPA by opportunity so offenses and players can be compared more fairly.

EPA / Play

EPA per play = Total EPA ÷ Number of Plays

Total EPA can favor teams or players with more opportunities. EPA per play instead measures the average expected scoring value created on each play, making it useful when comparing efficiency across different workloads.

Why EPA Per Play Matters

Efficiency and volume answer different questions.

Total EPA

Measures the total scoring value accumulated across all qualifying plays.

EPA Per Play

Measures the average value produced on each play.

High Volume

A high-volume offense can accumulate substantial total EPA even if efficiency is only moderate.

High Efficiency

Strong EPA per play can reveal an offense that consistently creates valuable outcomes even with fewer total plays.

What Is QB EPA?

Quarterback EPA evaluates the expected scoring value created during quarterback-involved plays.

Passing

Passing EPA

Evaluates how much expected scoring value is created or lost through passing plays.

Rushing

Quarterback Rushing EPA

Mobile quarterbacks can also create value through scrambles, designed carries, and goal-line rushing opportunities.

Context

Down and Distance

EPA rewards plays based on situational value rather than simply counting completions or passing yards.

Efficiency

QB EPA Per Play

EPA per play can help compare quarterback efficiency while accounting for differences in passing volume.

EPA vs Traditional Football Stats

EPA adds game context that basic box-score statistics cannot capture alone.

Passing Yards

Passing yards measure distance gained but do not account for down, distance, field position, or whether the drive became more valuable.

Completion Percentage

Completion percentage measures accuracy outcomes but does not distinguish between high-value and low-value completions.

Touchdowns

Touchdowns are extremely valuable outcomes but occur much less frequently than the individual plays that build a scoring drive.

EPA

EPA evaluates the change in expected scoring value created by each play within its specific situation.

EPA vs Success Rate

EPA measures the magnitude of play value while Success Rate focuses on how consistently positive outcomes occur.

EPA

A single explosive play can generate a large amount of positive EPA.

Success Rate

Success Rate measures how frequently an offense produces successful outcomes across its total play sample.

Explosiveness

EPA can capture the value of highly explosive scoring and field-position plays.

Consistency

Success Rate can help show whether efficiency is repeatable across many individual plays.

Related Metric

EPA and Success Rate work best together.

An offense can create strong EPA through explosive plays while remaining inconsistent from snap to snap. Combining the two metrics provides additional context around both efficiency and consistency.

01

Why Bettors Use EPA

EPA can provide context behind box-score production when evaluating teams, game environments, and player opportunities.

01

Identify Efficient Offenses

Efficient offenses may sustain more productive drives and create additional scoring and player-volume opportunities.

02

Identify Defensive Weakness

EPA allowed can help show whether a defense consistently permits opponents to improve expected scoring position.

03

Look Beyond Final Scores

Final scores can be influenced by turnovers, field position, special teams, and other events that may hide underlying efficiency.

04

Add Matchup Context

EPA should be combined with player usage, injuries, weather, pressure, and expected game script rather than used as a standalone signal.

EPA Football FAQ

Common questions about Expected Points Added and NFL efficiency analysis.

What does EPA stand for in football?

EPA stands for Expected Points Added. It measures how much a play increases or decreases a team's expected scoring value.

What is EPA in the NFL?

EPA is an efficiency metric used to evaluate the value created by individual plays based on their game situation, including down, distance, field position, and scoring context.

How is EPA calculated in football?

EPA is calculated by subtracting the expected-points value before a play from the expected-points value after the play.

What is EPA per play?

EPA per play divides total EPA by the number of plays in the sample. It helps compare efficiency while accounting for differences in volume.

Is higher EPA better?

For an offense, higher positive EPA generally indicates more efficient creation of expected scoring value. When evaluating EPA allowed by a defense, lower values generally indicate stronger defensive performance.

What is QB EPA?

QB EPA describes the expected scoring value created during plays involving the quarterback, including passing and quarterback rushing plays identified by the PlayMaker EPA dataset.

Is EPA better than yards?

EPA and yards answer different questions. Yards measure distance gained, while EPA measures how much a play changed the expected scoring outcome within its game situation.

Why do NFL bettors use EPA?

EPA can help bettors evaluate offensive and defensive efficiency beyond final scores and basic box-score totals. It is most useful when combined with matchup, usage, injury, weather, and game-script information.
