What Is Position Intelligence?

A structured breakdown of how different NFL roles create player production.

Core Idea

Every position follows a different path to production.

Quarterbacks depend on passing volume and pressure conditions. Running backs depend on carries, receiving work, and scoring-area usage. Wide receivers and tight ends depend on routes, targets, target depth, and coverage matchups.

Why Position-Specific Analysis Matters

Different positions require different workload and matchup signals.

01

Opportunity Is Different

Quarterbacks rely on attempts and dropbacks, running backs rely on touches, and receivers rely on routes and targets.

02

Efficiency Is Different

EPA, success rate, yards per carry, YPRR, catch rate, and YAC describe different types of performance.

03

Matchups Are Different

Pass rush affects quarterbacks differently than coverage affects receivers or front-seven strength affects running backs.

04

Game Script Is Different

Trailing teams may increase passing volume, while leading teams may create additional rushing opportunity.

Quarterback Path to Production

Quarterback output begins with opportunity and passing environment.

Passing Volume

Attempts, dropbacks, pace, and expected score flow establish the quarterback’s opportunity baseline.

Passing Efficiency

Completion rate, yards per attempt, EPA, and success rate refine expected production.

Pressure Environment

Defensive pressure and offensive line health affect timing, accuracy, and downfield passing.

Rushing Contribution

Designed carries, scrambles, and goal-line usage add another path to quarterback production.

Running Back Path to Production

Running back performance depends on workload, role, and game flow.

Carry Share

Team rushing distribution reveals whether the player has a lead role or operates inside a committee.

Receiving Work

Routes, targets, and passing-down usage can preserve opportunity in negative game scripts.

Red-Zone Role

Goal-line carries and red-zone targets provide higher-value scoring opportunities.

Run Matchup

Defensive front strength, tackling, and offensive line health affect rushing efficiency.

Wide Receiver Path to Production

Wide receiver output depends on routes, target volume, and target quality.

Route Participation

A receiver must consistently run routes to remain available for passing opportunities.

Target Share

Target share shows how much of the passing offense is directed toward the receiver.

Explore NFL Target Share Leaders →

Target Depth

Air Yards and average depth of target help describe the value and volatility of each opportunity.

Coverage Context

Alignment, cornerback matchups, safety support, and defensive pressure shape target quality.

Tight End Path to Production

Tight end analysis must separate total playing time from receiving involvement.

Route Participation

Total snaps can be misleading when the player remains in protection or blocks on passing plays.

Target Involvement

Target share and targets per route reveal how frequently the player earns receiving opportunity.

Red-Zone Usage

End-zone targets and designed scoring routes help explain touchdown opportunity.

Coverage Matchup

Linebackers, safeties, zone structure, and defensive pressure affect tight end routes.
