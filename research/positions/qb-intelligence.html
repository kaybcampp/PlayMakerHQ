QB Intelligence Quarterback Performance Model

QB Intelligence evaluates quarterback performance through passing volume, efficiency, pressure response, rushing ability, game environment, and defensive matchup context—not passing yards alone.

What QB Intelligence Measures

Quarterback production comes from the interaction between opportunity, efficiency, matchup quality, and game flow.

Core Idea

Passing volume creates opportunity. Efficiency and context determine the outcome.

A quarterback may receive more attempts in a trailing game, but pressure, coverage, weather, and offensive line performance still determine how productive those attempts become.

Core QB Metrics

These metrics establish the quarterback’s baseline role and performance profile.

01

Passing Volume

Attempts, completions, dropbacks, and recent passing workload establish the quarterback’s opportunity baseline.

02

EPA Efficiency

Expected Points Added measures how effectively quarterback plays improve the offense’s expected scoring position.

03

Success Rate

Success rate helps measure how consistently the passing offense produces useful plays rather than relying on isolated explosive gains.

04

Passing Efficiency

Completion rate, yards per attempt, touchdown rate, and interception rate help describe the quality of passing production.

Pressure and Pocket Performance

Defensive pressure can change the timing, depth, and efficiency of every pass play.

01

Pressure Rate

Measures how often the quarterback is hurried, hit, or sacked during passing plays.

02

Sack Conversion

Some quarterbacks avoid sacks under pressure, while others allow disruption to become lost-yardage plays.

03

Time to Throw

Faster release times can reduce pressure risk, while longer-developing concepts may require stronger protection.

04

Pressure Response

Accuracy, decision-making, scrambling, and turnover risk may change significantly when the pocket collapses.

QB Rushing Upside

Dual-threat production can add opportunity beyond traditional passing metrics.

01

Designed Runs

Quarterback draws, read-option plays, and other designed concepts create planned rushing volume.

02

Scramble Rate

Scrambles create additional rushing opportunities when passing plays break down.

03

Red-Zone Rushing

Quarterbacks with designed goal-line usage may carry additional rushing touchdown opportunity.

04

Rushing Floor

Stable rushing involvement can support total production when passing efficiency is limited.

Quarterback Matchup Factors

Defensive quality and game environment can change both passing volume and efficiency.

01

Defensive Pressure

High-pressure defenses can shorten throwing windows, increase sack risk, and disrupt deeper passing concepts.

02

Coverage Strength

Strong secondaries may reduce open targets, explosive completions, and receiver separation.

03

Pace of Play

Faster games can create more total possessions and increase potential quarterback dropbacks.

04

Weather and Venue

Wind, rain, temperature, and indoor conditions may affect passing efficiency and expected play selection.

Game Script and Passing Volume

Score differential and expected game flow often shape quarterback opportunity.

01

Trailing Script

Teams playing from behind often increase pass attempts as they try to close the scoring gap.

02

Leading Script

Teams protecting a lead may reduce passing volume and shift toward the running game.

03

Neutral Script

Competitive games may preserve the offense’s typical pass-run balance for longer periods.

04

Late-Game Volume

Urgency, clock management, and defensive coverage can create high-volume passing stretches late in games.

Why additional passing volume does not always mean better passing efficiency.

High-pressure defense plus a likely trailing game script

01

02

03

Environment Adjustment

04

Final Evaluation

PlayMaker compares the adjusted expectation with the listed line to support a ride, fade, or avoid decision.
