RB Intelligence Running Back Performance Model

RB Intelligence evaluates running back performance through rushing workload, snap share, receiving involvement, red-zone usage, efficiency, game script, and defensive matchup context—not rushing yards alone.

What RB Intelligence Measures

Running back production depends on workload, role, efficiency, scoring opportunity, and the way each game unfolds.

Core Idea

Touches create opportunity. Role and matchup determine how valuable those touches become.

Two running backs can record similar yardage while carrying very different workloads, receiving roles, red-zone usage, and future opportunity.

Core RB Metrics

These metrics establish a running back’s workload and role inside the offense.

01

Carry Share

Measures the percentage of team rushing attempts handled by the player and helps identify lead-back versus committee roles.

02

Snap Share

Shows how often the running back is on the field and whether the player has access to passing-down and situational work.

03

Touch Volume

Carries and receptions combine to create the player’s total opportunity baseline.

04

Rushing Efficiency

Yards per carry, success rate, explosive runs, and defensive context help describe how productive the workload has been.

Receiving Involvement

Passing-game usage can protect running back opportunity when rushing volume falls.

01

Target Share

Measures how much of the team’s passing volume is directed toward the running back.

02

Route Participation

A player must run routes consistently to earn repeatable receiving opportunity.

03

Checkdown Role

Quarterback tendencies and defensive pressure can create additional short-area targets for running backs.

04

Passing-Down Usage

Third-down and two-minute work can add receptions even when the player is not receiving the majority of carries.

Scoring and Red-Zone Usage

Total workload and scoring-area workload are not always the same.

01

Red-Zone Carries

Carries inside the 20-yard line provide higher-value touchdown opportunities.

02

Goal-Line Role

Carries near the goal line help identify which player the offense trusts in short-yardage scoring situations.

03

Red-Zone Targets

Receiving opportunities near the end zone can add another path to touchdowns.

04

Touchdown Variance

Scoring can fluctuate, so PlayMaker separates repeatable opportunity from short-term touchdown results.

Running Back Matchup Factors

Defensive quality and expected game flow influence both volume and efficiency.

01

Run Defense Strength

Defensive rushing efficiency, tackling, and front-seven strength help shape expected rushing production.

02

Offensive Line Health

Injuries and blocking matchups can change rushing lanes, contact timing, and short-yardage success.

03

Receiving Matchup

Linebacker coverage and defensive pressure can affect running back target and reception opportunity.

04

Pace of Play

Faster games may create more total possessions and additional opportunities for touches.

Game Script and RB Volume

Score differential can shift the balance between rushing and receiving work.

01

Leading Script

Teams protecting a lead often increase rushing volume and create clock-management carries.

02

Trailing Script

Negative game flow may reduce carries, especially for backs without a passing-down role.

03

Neutral Script

Competitive games may preserve the offense’s intended backfield rotation.

04

Role Versatility

Backs who handle carries and targets may remain involved across a wider range of game environments.

RB Intelligence Example

Why a strong workload profile matters more than one box-score result.

Example

Running back with a 70% carry share and consistent red-zone work

That player owns most of the team’s rushing opportunity and receives valuable scoring-area touches. Receiving involvement would add another layer of opportunity if the game turns pass-heavy.

How PlayMaker Uses RB Intelligence

PlayMaker combines workload, efficiency, player availability, and matchup context when evaluating running back props.

01

Recent Workload

Carries, targets, receptions, snaps, and recent backfield distribution establish the player’s current role.

02

Injury Adjustment

Teammate availability and offensive line injuries can change expected touches and efficiency.

03

Matchup Adjustment

Run-defense tiers, game script, pace, and receiving matchup help adjust the opportunity baseline.

04

Final Evaluation

The adjusted expectation is compared with the listed line to support a ride, fade, or avoid decision.
