TE Intelligence Tight End Performance Model

TE Intelligence evaluates tight end performance through route participation, target share, receiving efficiency, red-zone usage, blocking responsibility, game script, and defensive matchup context—not touchdowns alone.

What TE Intelligence Measures

Tight end production depends on whether the player is functioning as a true receiver, an in-line blocker, or a combination of both.

Core Idea

Snaps create availability. Routes and targets create receiving opportunity.

A tight end may play most offensive snaps without running enough routes to support consistent receiving production. PlayMaker separates total playing time from actual pass-catching opportunity.

Core TE Metrics

These metrics establish the tight end’s role inside the passing offense.

01

Route Participation

Measures how often the tight end runs a route when the offense calls a passing play.

02

Target Share

Shows the percentage of team passing targets directed toward the tight end.

03

Snap Share

Measures overall playing-time involvement, while providing context for route and blocking responsibilities.

04

Targets per Route

Helps show how frequently the player earns an opportunity when actually released into a route.

Receiving Role and Efficiency

Tight end usage can range from short-area checkdowns to high-value downfield and red-zone routes.

01

Average Target Depth

Shows whether the tight end is primarily used near the line of scrimmage or on deeper intermediate routes.

02

Catch Rate

Measures how often targets become receptions and helps describe the stability of the receiving role.

03

Yards per Route Run

Measures receiving production relative to actual route volume, rather than total snaps alone.

04

Yards After Catch

Shows how much production the tight end creates after securing the football.

Route Versus Blocking Responsibility

High snap volume does not always mean high receiving opportunity.

01

Pass Blocking

Tight ends may remain in protection against strong pass rushes, reducing their route volume.

02

Run Blocking

In-line tight ends may play significant snaps while being used primarily to support the rushing offense.

03

Formation Usage

Slot, wide, wing, and traditional in-line alignments can create different route and matchup opportunities.

04

Personnel Packages

Multiple-tight-end formations can divide routes and targets between several players.

Red-Zone and Scoring Role

Tight ends often receive high-value opportunities near the end zone.

01

Red-Zone Targets

Targets inside the 20-yard line provide increased touchdown opportunity.

02

End-Zone Targets

Direct targets in the end zone reveal whether the player is a designed scoring option.

03

First-Read Usage

Some tight ends are primary reads on designed scoring concepts, while others receive targets only after the play develops.

04

Touchdown Variance

Touchdowns can fluctuate, so scoring results should be evaluated alongside repeatable red-zone opportunity.

Tight End Matchup Factors

Defensive structure can affect route volume, target quality, and receiving efficiency.

01

Linebacker Coverage

Coverage ability, athleticism, and underneath zone responsibility can affect short and intermediate tight end routes.

02

Safety Matchups

Safeties may limit deeper routes and contested-catch opportunities in the middle of the field.

03

Defensive Pressure

Strong pass rushes can create quick targets, but they may also force tight ends to remain in protection.

04

Coverage Scheme

Man coverage, zone structure, and defensive attention can change where tight end targets develop.

Game Script and TE Volume

The flow of the game can affect both route participation and target opportunity.

01

Trailing Script

Pass-heavy game flow can create additional routes and short-area receiving opportunities.

02

Leading Script

Teams protecting a lead may use tight ends more heavily as run blockers and reduce overall passing volume.

03

Third Downs

Tight ends with reliable short-area roles may receive valuable targets on conversion attempts.

04

Two-Minute Offense

Hurry-up situations can create additional routes for tight ends trusted in the passing game.

TE Intelligence Example

Why receiving role matters more than total snap count alone.

Example

Tight end with 75% route participation and a 20% target share

That player is regularly involved as a receiver and earns a meaningful portion of the passing offense. Red-zone usage would add another layer of scoring opportunity.

How PlayMaker Uses TE Intelligence

PlayMaker combines receiving role, recent performance, matchup context, and scoring-area opportunity when evaluating tight end props.

01

Recent Usage

Routes, targets, receptions, yardage, snaps, and recent role changes establish the player’s current baseline.

02

Route Adjustment

PlayMaker distinguishes receiving routes from total snaps so blocking-heavy usage does not inflate expectations.

03

Matchup Adjustment

Defensive pressure, coverage structure, red-zone defense, and expected game flow help adjust the usage baseline.

04

Final Evaluation

The adjusted expectation is compared with the listed line to support a ride, fade, or avoid decision.
