WR Intelligence Wide Receiver Performance Model

WR Intelligence evaluates wide receiver performance through target volume, route participation, air yards, receiving efficiency, yards after catch, red-zone usage, game script, and defensive matchup context—not receptions alone.

What WR Intelligence Measures

Wide receiver production depends on opportunity, route role, target quality, efficiency, and the environment surrounding each pass play.

Core Idea

Routes create availability. Targets and air yards define receiving opportunity.

A receiver can run nearly every route without earning consistent targets, while another player may command high-value opportunities on fewer total routes. PlayMaker evaluates the complete usage profile.

Core WR Metrics

These metrics establish a wide receiver’s role inside the passing offense.

01

Target Share

Measures the percentage of team passing targets directed toward the wide receiver.

02

Route Participation

Shows how often the receiver runs a route when the offense calls a passing play.

03

Air Yards

Measures the downfield distance of a player’s targets and helps describe the value of the receiving opportunity.

04

Targets per Route

Shows how frequently the receiver earns a target when actively participating in a route.

Route Role and Alignment

Alignment and route responsibility influence the type of targets a receiver earns.

01

Outside Alignment

Outside receivers may see more boundary routes, contested targets, and downfield opportunities.

02

Slot Alignment

Slot receivers may benefit from shorter releases, inside leverage, and higher-percentage route concepts.

03

Route Depth

Short, intermediate, and deep routes create different reception and yardage profiles.

04

Motion and Formation

Pre-snap motion and formation placement can help create releases, favorable matchups, and designed touches.

Receiving Efficiency

Efficiency shows how much production the receiver creates from available routes and targets.

01

Yards per Route Run

Measures receiving yards relative to route participation and helps compare production across different workloads.

02

Catch Rate

Shows how often targets become receptions, while reflecting target depth and quarterback accuracy.

03

Yards per Target

Measures total receiving production relative to the number of opportunities earned.

04

Success Rate

Helps distinguish repeatable receiving efficiency from production driven by isolated explosive plays.

Explosive Play Ability

Downfield targets and post-catch production can create large receiving outcomes.

01

Deep Target Rate

Measures how frequently the receiver earns high-value downfield opportunities.

02

Yards After Catch

Shows how much receiving production is created after the player secures the football.

03

Separation

Route technique, speed, scheme, and coverage can all influence the space available at the catch point.

04

Big-Play Dependency

PlayMaker considers whether recent production is supported by stable volume or dependent on a small number of explosive catches.

Red-Zone and Scoring Role

Scoring-area usage provides context beyond total targets and receiving yards.

01

Red-Zone Targets

Targets inside the 20-yard line provide increased touchdown opportunity.

02

End-Zone Targets

Direct targets in the end zone help identify designed scoring involvement.

03

First-Read Usage

Some receivers are primary reads on scoring concepts, while others depend on later route progression.

04

Touchdown Variance

Touchdowns can fluctuate, so results should be evaluated alongside repeatable red-zone opportunity.

Wide Receiver Matchup Factors

Coverage, pressure, alignment, and defensive structure can change both target quality and efficiency.

01

Coverage Quality

Cornerback ability and team coverage structure can affect separation, catch probability, and route timing.

02

Slot Versus Outside

Receiver alignment determines which defenders and coverage responsibilities are most relevant.

03

Defensive Pressure

Strong pass rushes can shorten throwing windows and reduce time for deeper routes to develop.

04

Safety Support

Help coverage over the top can reduce deep opportunities or redirect targets toward shorter areas of the field.

Game Script and WR Volume

Expected game flow can influence team passing volume and individual receiving opportunity.

01

Trailing Script

Teams playing from behind often increase pass attempts, creating more available receiver targets.

02

Leading Script

Teams protecting a lead may reduce passing volume as they shift toward the running game.

03

Neutral Script

Competitive games may preserve normal passing tendencies and route distribution for longer periods.

04

Late-Game Volume

Hurry-up situations can create additional routes, targets, and short-area completions.

WR Intelligence Example

Why usage quality matters alongside total receiving production.

Example

Wide receiver with a 30% target share and consistent downfield usage

That receiver owns a large portion of the passing offense and receives valuable air-yard opportunity. Coverage strength, quarterback pressure, and catch efficiency still determine how much of that opportunity becomes production.

How PlayMaker Uses WR Intelligence

PlayMaker combines volume, efficiency, recent performance, and matchup context when evaluating wide receiver props.

01

Recent Usage

Targets, receptions, routes, receiving yards, and recent role changes establish the player’s current baseline.

02

Opportunity Quality

Target depth, air yards, red-zone usage, and quarterback tendencies help describe target value.

03

Matchup Adjustment

Coverage quality, defensive pressure, game script, weather, and injuries help adjust the receiving baseline.

04

Final Evaluation

The adjusted expectation is compared with the listed line to support a ride, fade, or avoid decision.
