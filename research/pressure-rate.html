What is Pressure Rate? Pass Rush Impact Explained

Pressure Rate measures how often a defense disrupts the quarterback on passing plays. It helps evaluate how pass-rush strength may affect quarterback efficiency, sacks, turnovers, and receiving production.

Pressure Rate Definition

Measuring how frequently a defense disrupts the pocket.

Definition

Pressure Rate = quarterback pressures divided by opposing dropbacks.

Pressures may include sacks, quarterback hits, and hurries. Unlike sack rate alone, pressure rate captures pass-rush disruption even when the quarterback gets the ball away.

Why Pressure Rate Matters for Props

Defensive pressure can change the timing and efficiency of an entire passing offense.

01

Passing Efficiency

Frequent pressure can force shorter throws, rushed decisions, and lower completion efficiency.

02

Sack Risk

Strong pass-rush environments increase the chance of sacks, lost yardage, and stalled drives.

03

Turnover Potential

Disrupted pockets can lead to forced throws, interceptions, strip sacks, and other negative plays.

04

Receiver Opportunity

Pressure can reduce time for deeper routes to develop and redirect volume toward short-area receivers.

Pressure Rate Example

Why sacks do not tell the entire story.

Example

Defense A: 40% pressure rate but only two sacks

Even with only two sacks, the defense consistently disrupted the quarterback. That pressure may still lower passing efficiency, shorten target depth, and reduce the offense’s ability to sustain drives.

What Changes Pressure Rate?

Pass-rush performance depends on both the defense and the opposing offense.

01

Defensive Front

Elite edge rushers and interior defenders can generate pressure without requiring additional blitzers.

02

Blitz Frequency

Defenses can increase disruption by sending additional rushers, although blitzing may also create coverage vulnerabilities.

03

Offensive Line Quality

Injuries, weak individual matchups, and communication problems can increase pressure allowed.

04

Quarterback Style

Pocket movement, release time, scrambling ability, and decision-making all affect how often pressure becomes a negative play.

How Pressure Affects Different Positions

Pressure can shift production across the entire passing offense.

QB

Quarterbacks

Pressure can reduce completion rate, yards per attempt, passing touchdowns, and overall pocket stability.

WR

Wide Receivers

Deep and intermediate routes may suffer when the quarterback has less time to throw.

TE

Tight Ends

Tight ends may receive more short-area opportunities, but some may also stay in to help block.

RB

Running Backs

Checkdowns and screen passes may increase when pressure forces quarterbacks to release the ball quickly.

How PlayMaker Uses Pressure Rate

Pressure rate helps adjust passing expectations for specific defensive matchups.

01

02

04

Prop Evaluation
