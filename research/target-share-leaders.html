NFL Target Share Leaders 2026 Track Who Commands the Passing Game

Target share measures how much of a team's passing opportunity is directed toward a specific player. Use this guide to track NFL target share leaders, compare usage by position, understand recent trends, and evaluate how receiving opportunity can influence player prop research.

What Is Target Share?

Target share measures the percentage of a team's passing targets directed toward one player.

Definition

Target Share = Player Targets ÷ Team Pass Targets

If a team records 40 pass targets and one receiver earns 12 of them, that player owns a 30% target share. The metric helps measure how heavily the passing offense relies on that player regardless of the final number of catches or receiving yards.

How Target Share Is Calculated

Target share turns raw target volume into a percentage of the team's total passing opportunity.

01

Count Player Targets

Start with the number of official pass targets credited to the player during the selected game or sample.

02

Count Team Targets

Measure the total number of passes directed toward eligible receivers in the offense.

03

Divide Player By Team

Divide the player's targets by total team targets to determine the player's share of passing volume.

04

Convert To Percentage

Multiply the result by 100 to express the player's usage as a target share percentage.

Target Share Formula Example

A simple example shows how receiving opportunity can be compared across different offenses.

Example

12 player targets ÷ 40 team targets = 30% target share.

A player with a 30% target share is receiving nearly one out of every three passing opportunities in the offense. Even if that player finishes with limited yardage in a particular game, the underlying opportunity can remain strong.

Target Share Versus Raw Targets

Two players can have the same number of targets but very different roles within their offenses.

Example

Ten targets can mean very different things depending on team passing volume.

If Player A earns 10 targets on a team with 30 total targets, the player owns a 33.3% target share. If Player B earns 10 targets on a team with 50 total targets, the player owns only a 20% target share. Raw volume is equal, but offensive importance is not.

Target Share Versus Receptions

Targets measure opportunity while receptions measure completed outcomes.

Targets

Count how many passing attempts are directed toward the player.

Target Share

Measures the player's portion of total team targets.

Receptions

Measure how many targets are successfully completed.

Catch Rate

Measures how efficiently a player converts targets into receptions.

High Target Share vs Low Target Share

Target share helps identify how involved a player is within the structure of an offense.

High Usage

Featured Pass Catchers

Players with consistently high target share are usually primary receiving options who are heavily involved regardless of matchup.

Medium Usage

Secondary Options

These players may still provide value but often depend more heavily on matchup, injuries, or offensive game plan.

Low Usage

Limited Roles

Players with smaller target shares typically require efficiency, touchdowns, or unusual game environments to produce strong results.

Trend

Changing Roles

Increasing or decreasing target share can reveal developing roles before traditional statistics fully reflect the change.

What Changes A Player's Target Share?

Target share is influenced by offensive role, personnel changes, injuries, and game environment.

01

Injuries Around The Offense

When a major receiving option misses time, remaining players can absorb additional targets.

02

Route Participation

Players who are consistently running routes are more likely to receive passing opportunities.

03

Offensive Scheme

Pass-heavy offenses naturally create more opportunities for receivers throughout the season.

04

Quarterback Tendencies

Quarterbacks often develop preferred targets based on trust, timing, and offensive design.
