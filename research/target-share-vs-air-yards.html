Target Share vs Air Yards Volume vs Downfield Opportunity

Target Share tells you how often a player is being targeted. Air Yards tell you how far downfield those opportunities are occurring. Evaluating both together creates a clearer picture of receiving opportunity.

What Is the Difference Between Target Share and Air Yards?

One measures how often a receiver is involved. The other measures how deep those targets are.

Core Idea

Target Share = how often. Air Yards = how deep.

A receiver can command a large percentage of team targets without receiving many downfield opportunities. Another player may receive fewer targets but earn much deeper attempts. Looking at both metrics helps reveal receiving roles that raw target totals cannot explain by themselves.

Target Share vs Air Yards at a Glance

The two metrics answer different questions about a receiver's role.

Target Share

Measures Receiving Volume

Target Share measures the percentage of a team's passing targets directed toward a specific player.

It helps show how important that receiver is within the overall passing offense.

Why Target Share and Air Yards Work Better Together

Volume without target depth can be misleading, and target depth without volume can be too.

01

Volume

Target Share shows whether a player consistently receives opportunities within the passing offense.

02

Target Depth

Air Yards show whether those opportunities occur near the line of scrimmage or farther downfield.

03

Receiving Role

Combining the metrics helps distinguish possession receivers, intermediate options, deep threats, and low-volume receivers.

04

Prop Context

Receptions and receiving-yard props can depend on very different combinations of target volume and downfield opportunity.

Four Target Share and Air Yards Profiles

Combining volume and depth creates four useful receiver profiles.

High + High

High Target Share + High Air Yards

This receiver commands a large portion of team passing volume while also earning meaningful downfield opportunities.

The profile combines volume and target depth, creating multiple paths to receiving production.

High + Low

High Target Share + Low Air Yards

This receiver gets frequent targets, but many of those opportunities occur closer to the line of scrimmage.

The profile can support reception volume, while receiving-yard production may depend more heavily on YAC.

Low + High

Low Target Share + High Air Yards

This receiver gets fewer opportunities, but those targets tend to occur much farther downfield.

The profile can create explosive receiving games, but production may be more volatile because target volume is limited.

Low + Low

Low Target Share + Low Air Yards

This player receives a smaller portion of team targets without meaningful downfield opportunity.

The role generally creates fewer independent paths to large receiving-yard production.

Receiving Volume

What Air Yards Tell You

Air Yards add target-depth context to raw receiving volume.

Target Depth

Deeper targets create more yardage potential per opportunity.

A receiver earning significant Air Yards can generate large receiving totals from fewer completed passes. The tradeoff is that deeper targets can also create greater week-to-week volatility.

Air Yards and Receiving-Yard Props

Receiving-yard opportunity depends on more than target count.

Depth

Downfield Targets

Deeper targets carry more potential yardage before the receiver even catches the football.

Volume

Target Frequency

A deep target can be valuable, but low target volume can make production more dependent on a small number of plays.

Efficiency

Catch Conversion

Air Yards represent opportunity, not guaranteed production. The pass still needs to be completed.

YAC

Production After the Catch

Shorter-target receivers can still produce strong yardage when they consistently create yards after the catch.

Why Changes in Target Share and Air Yards Matter

Falling

Falling Target Share

Declining Target Share can signal increased competition, a role change, or reduced involvement.

Deeper

Rising Air Yards

A receiver may keep similar target volume while earning deeper and potentially more valuable opportunities.

Shorter

Falling Air Yards

A shift toward shorter routes can change the player's receptions and receiving-yard profile even if targets remain stable.

Target Share vs Air Yards Example

Two receivers can have the same number of targets and very different opportunity profiles.

Example

Ten targets do not always mean the same thing.

One receiver may earn ten short-area targets designed to create receptions and YAC opportunities. Another may earn ten deep targets that carry more yardage upside but a lower completion probability. Target count alone does not capture that difference. Air Yards add the missing target-depth context.

Target Share vs Air Yards FAQ

Common questions about NFL receiving volume and target depth.

What is the difference between Target Share and Air Yards?

Target Share measures the percentage of team targets directed toward a receiver. Air Yards measure how far downfield those targets travel. Target Share describes volume, while Air Yards describe target depth.

Is Target Share more important than Air Yards?

Neither metric is universally more important. Target Share helps measure receiving volume, while Air Yards add context about the depth and potential value of those opportunities.

Can a player have high Target Share but low Air Yards?

Yes. Short-area and possession receivers can receive a large share of team targets while operating closer to the line of scrimmage.

Can a player have low Target Share but high Air Yards?

Yes. Deep-threat receivers may receive fewer targets but accumulate substantial Air Yards because those opportunities occur farther downfield.

Is Target Share useful for reception props?

Target Share can provide useful context for reception props because it measures how frequently a player receives passing opportunities. Catch rate, matchup, route role, and quarterback play also matter.

Are Air Yards useful for receiving-yard props?

Air Yards can help describe the downfield value of a player's opportunities. Receiving-yard production also depends on completions, Target Share, YAC, matchup quality, and overall passing volume.

How does YAC relate to Air Yards?

Air Yards measure target depth before the catch, while YAC measures the yards gained after the reception. Both can contribute to receiving-yard production in different ways.

Which profile is best for receiving-yard props?

There is no single best profile in every matchup. High Target Share combined with meaningful Air Yards can provide both volume and downfield opportunity, while other receivers may create yardage through deep targets or strong YAC production.
