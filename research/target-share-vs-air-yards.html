Core Efficiency Metrics

Metrics that measure how effectively teams and players create value.

EPA

EPA stands for Expected Points Added. It measures how much a play increases or decreases a team's expected scoring value based on the game situation before and after the play.

Explore EPA →

EPA Per Play

EPA per play divides total Expected Points Added by the number of plays in a sample. It helps compare efficiency across teams or players with different workloads.

Learn About EPA Per Play →

Success Rate

Success Rate measures how consistently an offense produces positive or successful outcomes rather than relying only on a few explosive plays.

Explore Success Rate →

Offensive Efficiency

Offensive efficiency describes how effectively an offense turns possessions and plays into valuable outcomes such as first downs, field position, and scoring opportunities.

Explore Offensive EPA →

Receiving and Usage Metrics

Metrics that describe how often receivers are used and what kind of opportunities they receive.

Target Share

Target Share measures the percentage of a team's passing targets directed toward a specific player. It is one of the clearest ways to measure receiving opportunity.

View Target Share Leaders →

First-Read Target Share

First-Read Target Share measures how often a player is the quarterback's primary intended option within the designed passing concept.

Explore Target Share →

Air Yards

Air Yards measure how far the football travels through the air before reaching the intended receiver. The metric helps describe target depth and downfield opportunity.

Explore Air Yards →

YAC

YAC stands for Yards After Catch. It measures the number of yards a receiver gains after completing a catch.

Explore YAC →

Receiving Yards

Receiving Yards are the yards credited to a player after completed passes. They include both the distance the ball travels to the receiver and any yards gained after the catch.

Explore Receiving Players →

Scrimmage Yards

Scrimmage Yards combine a player's rushing yards and receiving yards. The stat is commonly used to measure total offensive production from running backs and other skill players.

Explore Player Production →

Receptions

Receptions count completed passes caught by a player. The stat measures completed receiving volume rather than total passing opportunity.

Compare Receiving Usage →

Catch Rate

Catch Rate measures the percentage of a player's targets that become completed receptions. It adds efficiency context to raw target volume.

Explore Target Context →

Passing and Pressure Metrics

Metrics used to evaluate quarterback efficiency and defensive disruption.

Pressure Rate

Pressure Rate measures how frequently a quarterback is pressured during passing opportunities. Pressures commonly include sacks, hits, and hurries.

Explore Pressure Rate →

Sack Rate

Sack Rate measures how frequently passing opportunities end with the quarterback being sacked. It captures only sacks rather than all forms of pressure.

Compare Pressure vs Sack Rate →

Blitz Rate

Blitz Rate measures how frequently a defense sends additional pass rushers. A high blitz rate does not automatically mean a high pressure rate.

Compare Pressure vs Blitz Rate →

Completion Percentage

Completion Percentage measures the percentage of passing attempts completed by a quarterback.

Passing Yards

Passing Yards measure the total yards gained through completed passes, including yards the receiver gains after the catch.

Explore Passing Metrics →

Dropback

A dropback is a passing play in which the quarterback retreats or sets up to pass. Pressure metrics are commonly measured against dropback volume.

Usage and Matchup Metrics

Metrics that describe player involvement and the environment surrounding a game.

Game Script

Game Script describes the expected or actual flow of a football game. Teams playing from behind often throw more, while teams protecting leads may rely more heavily on the run.

Snap Share

Snap Share measures the percentage of a team's offensive or defensive plays in which a player is on the field.

Red-Zone Usage

Red-Zone Usage describes how frequently a player receives carries, targets, or other opportunities near the opponent's goal line.

Defensive Matchup

A Defensive Matchup evaluates how an opposing defense may affect a player or position based on efficiency, pressure, coverage, rushing strength, and other context.

A Defensive Tier groups defenses into broader strength levels so matchup difficulty can be interpreted quickly.

Opportunity refers to the volume of chances a player receives to produce statistics, including carries, targets, routes, snaps, and scoring opportunities.

NFL Statistical Glossary FAQ

Quick answers to common football analytics questions.

What does EPA mean in football?

EPA stands for Expected Points Added. It measures how much a football play changes a team's expected scoring value.

What does YAC mean in football?

YAC stands for Yards After Catch. It measures how many yards a player gains after completing a reception.

What does target share mean in football?

Target Share measures the percentage of a team's passing targets directed toward a specific player.

What are scrimmage yards?

Scrimmage Yards combine a player's rushing yards and receiving yards into one total offensive yardage statistic.

What is pressure rate in football?

Pressure Rate measures how frequently a quarterback faces defensive pressure during passing opportunities.

What is game script in football?

Game Script describes how the score and expected flow of a game affect play calling, passing volume, rushing volume, and player opportunity.

What is the difference between target share and air yards?

Target Share measures how frequently a player is targeted, while Air Yards measure how far those intended passes travel downfield. Together they provide a broader view of receiving opportunity.
