NFL Yards After Catch Leaders 2026 YAC Stats & Post-Catch Production

NFL yards after catch, commonly called YAC, measures how many yards a player gains after securing a reception. Use this 2026 YAC research guide to understand yards after catch stats, compare post-catch production, evaluate explosive receiving ability, and add context to NFL player prop research.

What Is Yards After Catch?

Yards after catch measures the portion of receiving production created after the football is secured.

Definition

YAC = Receiving Yards Gained After the Catch

If a player catches a pass five yards beyond the line of scrimmage and gains another 12 yards before being tackled, those additional 12 yards are credited as yards after catch.

How Yards After Catch Is Created

YAC depends on more than raw receiving volume.

01

Catch the Football

Yards after catch begins only after a completed reception is secured.

02

Create Space

Route design, coverage and ball placement can determine how much open field is available immediately after the catch.

03

Break or Avoid Tackles

Speed, balance, strength and open-field movement can turn short receptions into larger gains.

04

Add Post-Catch Yardage

Every yard gained after possession is added to the player's YAC total.

Yards After Catch Example

Short targets can still create explosive receiving production.

Example

5 Air Yards + 20 Yards After Catch = 25 Receiving Yards

A receiver does not need a deep target to create a large receiving gain. Strong YAC production can turn screens, slants, crossing routes and checkdowns into explosive plays.

YAC vs Air Yards

Air yards and yards after catch describe different parts of receiving production.

Air

Air Yards

Air yards measure how far downfield a target travels before reaching the receiver.

YAC

Yards After Catch

YAC measures what the player creates after the reception is completed.

Deep

Vertical Production

Some receivers generate yardage primarily from deep targets and large air-yard opportunities.

Short

Post-Catch Production

Other receivers create large gains through shorter targets followed by strong open-field production.

YAC vs Target Share

Opportunity and efficiency answer different questions.

Usage vs Production

Target Share Measures Opportunity. YAC Measures Post-Catch Production.

A player can command heavy receiving volume without producing large YAC, while another player may create explosive yardage from fewer targets. Use NFL target share alongside YAC to separate receiving opportunity from post-catch efficiency.

YAC helps explain how receivers turn opportunities into additional production.

01

02

High YAC vs Low YAC

Different receiving roles naturally create different post-catch opportunities.

High YAC

Open-Field Playmakers

Players with strong YAC production may benefit from speed, broken tackles, favorable route concepts or manufactured space.

Low YAC

Catch-Point Production

Lower YAC does not automatically mean poor receiving performance. Downfield receivers often secure catches near defenders with limited room after the reception.

Role

Route Type Matters

Screens and crossers naturally create different YAC opportunities than sideline routes, fades and contested downfield targets.

Context

Evaluate the Entire Receiving Role

Yards After Catch by Position

Wide receivers, tight ends and running backs create YAC in different ways.

WR

Wide Receiver YAC

Wide receivers can generate YAC through slants, crossers, screens and other routes designed to create space after the catch.

TE

Tight End YAC

Tight ends often create post-catch production through size, strength and favorable matchups against linebackers or defensive backs.

RB

Running Back YAC

Running backs frequently receive checkdowns and screens with open space in front of them.

Role

Usage Matters

Position alone does not determine YAC. Route depth, target type, personnel usage and offensive design all influence post-catch opportunity.

NFL Yards After Catch FAQ

Common questions about YAC and post-catch receiving production.

What does YAC mean in football?

YAC stands for yards after catch. It measures how many receiving yards a player gains after completing a reception.

What is yards after catch?

Yards after catch measures the distance a player gains after securing the football until the play ends.

Why is YAC important in the NFL?

YAC helps identify players who create additional receiving production after the catch through speed, strength, open-field movement and offensive design.

Is high YAC always better?

Not necessarily. Receiving roles create different YAC opportunities. Deep receivers may produce significant yardage before the catch while short-area players can rely more heavily on post-catch production.

What is the difference between YAC and air yards?

Air yards measure how far a target travels before reaching the player. YAC measures the yardage created after the reception is completed.

Why does YAC matter for receiving props?

YAC can materially increase receiving-yard outcomes because players can generate additional production after relatively short completions.
