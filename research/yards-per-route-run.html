NFL Yards Per Route Run Leaders 2026 YPRR Stats & Receiving Efficiency

NFL yards per route run, commonly called YPRR, measures how many receiving yards a player generates for every route they run. Use this 2026 YPRR guide to understand route-level receiving efficiency, compare player production, evaluate receiving roles, and add context to NFL player prop research.

What Is Yards Per Route Run?

YPRR measures receiving production relative to how often a player actually runs a route.

Definition

YPRR = Receiving Yards ÷ Routes Run

Unlike yards per reception, YPRR evaluates every route a player runs, including routes where the player is not targeted. This provides a broader view of how efficiently a receiver turns route participation into production.

How YPRR Is Calculated

Yards per route run combines total receiving production with route participation.

01

Count Routes Run

Measure the number of pass routes a player runs over the selected game or sample.

02

Measure Receiving Yards

Add the player's total receiving yards over the same sample.

03

Divide Yards by Routes

Divide receiving yards by total routes run.

04

Compare Efficiency

The resulting YPRR value makes route-level receiving efficiency easier to compare across players and roles.

YPRR Formula Example

Route volume provides important context around receiving production.

Example

84 Receiving Yards ÷ 30 Routes = 2.8 YPRR

If another receiver produces 42 yards across the same 30 routes, that player records 1.4 YPRR. The first receiver generated twice as much receiving production per route.

YPRR vs Yards Per Reception

Both metrics measure receiving efficiency, but they use different opportunity denominators.

YPRR

Yards Per Route Run

Measures receiving yards across every route the player runs, whether targeted or not.

YPR

Yards Per Reception

Measures receiving yards only across completed receptions.

Routes

Opportunity Context

YPRR includes route participation, making it more sensitive to how often a player is actually involved in passing plays.

Catch

Catch Efficiency

Yards per reception focuses on production after catches occur and does not account for non-targeted routes.

YPRR vs Target Share

Target volume and route efficiency describe different parts of a receiver's role.

Volume + Efficiency

Target Share Measures Opportunity. YPRR Measures Production Per Route.

A player can command a large portion of team targets while producing modest efficiency, while another receiver may generate strong YPRR on fewer opportunities. Compare yards per route run with NFL target share to evaluate volume and efficiency together.

YPRR vs Air Yards

Downfield opportunity and route-level production answer different questions.

Depth + Efficiency

Air Yards Measure Target Depth. YPRR Measures Receiving Output Per Route.

A receiver can earn valuable deep targets without converting them into consistent production. Compare YPRR with NFL Air Yards to understand both the quality of downfield opportunity and how efficiently a player converts routes into receiving yards.

NFL YPRR FAQ

Common questions about yards per route run and receiving efficiency.

What does YPRR mean in football?

YPRR stands for yards per route run. It measures how many receiving yards a player produces for every route they run.

How is yards per route run calculated?

Divide a player's total receiving yards by the number of routes they run over the same sample.

What is a good YPRR?

YPRR should be evaluated relative to position, role, route volume, target volume, and sample size rather than using one universal threshold for every player.

What is the difference between YPRR and yards per reception?

YPRR uses every route as the denominator. Yards per reception uses only completed catches.

Why does YPRR matter for wide receivers?

YPRR helps show how efficiently a receiver converts route participation into receiving yardage.

Why does YPRR matter for player props?

Route-level efficiency can add context to receiving-yard projections, especially when combined with target share, playing time, matchup conditions, and recent role trends.
